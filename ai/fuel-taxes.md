# Fuel Taxes

## Purpose

This document explains the fuel tax revenue sources that fund Walton County's Transportation Fund.

## Overview

Fuel taxes are a category of General Government Taxes restricted to transportation purposes, funding the Transportation Fund. See [Revenue Guide](revenue-guide.md) and [Fund Descriptions](fund-descriptions.md).

## Types of Fuel Tax Revenue

- **Local Option Fuel Tax**: a locally levied tax on motor fuel, authorized under Florida law, restricted to transportation-related uses (roadway construction, maintenance, drainage, and infrastructure operations).
- **State Fuel Taxes / State Revenue Share Proceeds**: state-collected fuel tax revenue shared with counties by statutory formula.
- **Constitutional Fuel Tax, County Fuel Tax, Municipal Fuel Tax**: additional state-shared fuel tax revenue streams, each governed by its own statutory distribution formula, all restricted to transportation purposes.

## Why This Revenue Is Restricted

Fuel tax revenue is legally restricted to transportation infrastructure under Florida law — it cannot be redirected to unrelated County services such as parks, libraries, or general administration, regardless of Board preference. This is the same legal-restriction principle that governs the Tourist Development Tax; see [Fund Structure](fund-structure.md).

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can fuel tax revenue be used for anything besides roads?** No — it is legally restricted to transportation purposes.

**Why are there multiple, separately named fuel taxes?** Each reflects a different statutory authorization and distribution formula (local option vs. various state-shared fuel tax programs), though all are restricted to transportation use.

**Does new development also contribute to transportation funding?** Yes, through transportation impact fees and the Mobility Fee Fund, in addition to fuel tax revenue. See [Impact Fees](impact-fees.md).

## Common Misconceptions

- **"Gas tax revenue is just general county revenue."** It is legally restricted to transportation purposes, distinct from the General Fund's more flexible ad valorem tax revenue.
