# Fund Descriptions

## Purpose

This document describes the purpose, revenue sources, and typical uses of each of Walton County's 20 funds. See [Fund Structure](fund-structure.md) for why these funds exist and how they're categorized.

## Major Governmental Funds

### General Fund (001)
The County's primary operating fund. It accounts for financial resources not required to be reported in another fund, with major funding from ad valorem (property) taxes. The collection and disbursement of monies to the Constitutional Officers is also accounted for in this fund.

### Transportation Fund (101)
Accounts for motor fuel taxes, local option gas taxes, state revenue sharing, and grants supporting roadway construction, maintenance, drainage, and infrastructure operations.

### Sheriff Fund (107)
Accounts for fines, forfeitures, grants, and transfers supporting Sheriff's Office operations in accordance with Florida Statutes.

### Tourist Development Fund (111)
Supports tourism promotion, beach renourishment, visitor-serving infrastructure, and public safety initiatives, funded primarily by the Tourist Development Tax. See [Tourist Development Tax](tourist-development-tax.md).

### Solid Waste Fund (112)
Accounts for landfill operations, funded through County surtaxes, state grants, landfill fees, and scrap sales revenue.

### Capital Projects Fund (300)
Accounts for resources designated for acquiring, constructing, and improving major capital facilities and infrastructure. See [Capital Projects](capital-projects.md).

## Non-Major Governmental Funds

- **Debt Service Fund (201)**: accounts for the accumulation of resources for, and payment of, long-term debt principal and interest.
- **MSBU Fund (102)**: special assessments for infrastructure improvements within a Municipal Service Benefit Unit — a defined area where property owners are assessed for a specific local improvement.
- **Building Fund (103)**: fees and assessments supporting building compliance and permitting activities.
- **State Housing Initiative Program (SHIP) Fund (104)**: State Housing Initiatives Partnership Program grant resources supporting affordable housing programs.
- **Mosquito Control Fund (105)**: dependent district activities for mosquito and insect control.
- **Mosquito Control State Aid Fund (106)**: state grant resources supporting mosquito and insect control.
- **Law Enforcement Trust Fund (108)**: resources restricted to law enforcement purposes under Florida Statutes governing forfeiture proceeds.
- **E911 Fund (109)**: phone charges assessed for emergency assistance (911) communications services.
- **Housing & Urban Development Fund (110)**: federal Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) grant resources.
- **Preservation Fund (113)**: fees paid in lieu of reserved land, used for restoration of native vegetation on public lands.
- **Recreation Plat Fee Fund (114)**: developer assessments supporting recreational facilities.
- **Sidewalk Fund (115)**: assessments paid in lieu of constructing required sidewalks.
- **Mobility Fee Fund (116)**: fees supporting multi-modal transportation infrastructure tied to new development.

## Internal Service Fund

### Self-Insurance Fund (503)
An Internal Service Fund used to account for the financing of goods or services provided by one County department to other departments on a cost-reimbursement basis — in this case, self-insurance activities. This is a Proprietary Fund, not a Governmental Fund, because it operates on a cost-recovery, exchange-transaction basis rather than through tax revenue.

## Fund Types At A Glance

- **Governmental Funds**: core public services — general operations, transportation, public safety, tourism, capital projects, grants, assessments, and other restricted activities.
- **Proprietary Funds**: business-type or internal service activities, where costs are recovered through user charges or internal allocations.
- **Fiduciary Funds**: resources held by the County on behalf of other individuals, agencies, governments, or funds.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Which fund receives most of the County's property tax revenue?**
The General Fund — "the majority of funding is from ad valorem taxes."

**Can the Tourist Development Fund pay for a Sheriff's Office patrol car?**
Generally no, unless the specific use ties directly to the statutorily authorized purposes of the Tourist Development Tax (tourism promotion, beach/shoreline maintenance, and related visitor-impact public safety needs). Routine county-wide law enforcement is primarily funded through the Sheriff Fund and General Fund. See [Tourist Development Tax](tourist-development-tax.md) and [Common Misconceptions](common-misconceptions.md).

**What is an MSBU, and why would my property be assessed one?**
A Municipal Service Benefit Unit is a defined geographic area where property owners are assessed for a specific local improvement or service (such as road paving or drainage) that specifically benefits that area, rather than being funded countywide.

**Why does the E911 Fund exist separately from the Sheriff Fund, if the Sheriff's Office operates the E911 Communications Center?**
The E911 Fund tracks the specific 911-service phone charge revenue that legally must fund emergency communications infrastructure and operations, even though the Sheriff's Office is the department that operates the center day to day. Fund separation keeps that dedicated revenue's use transparent regardless of which department carries out the service. See [Department to Fund Relationship (live page)](/pages/department-to-fund-relationship.html).

## Common Misconceptions

- **"MSBU funds are secret extra taxes."** MSBU assessments fund specific, locally voted or petitioned improvements benefiting the assessed area directly, and are a standard Florida local government financing tool, not an additional general tax.
- **"The Self-Insurance Fund is 'profit' the County keeps."** It exists to pool and manage risk/claims costs across departments on a cost-recovery basis, not to generate revenue.
