# Budget Terminology

## Purpose

This document defines the core budget terms used throughout this site in plain English. It covers the terms most essential to correctly interpreting a budget figure. The site's [full glossary](/pages/glossary-acronyms-and-frequently-asked-questions.html) defines 112 terms and acronyms in total — this document is a curated subset for quick reference; defer to the full glossary page for any term not covered here.

## Budget Status Terms

- **Budget**: the financial plan of revenues and expenditures the Board of County Commissioners approves for a fiscal year.
- **Adopted Budget**: "The financial plan of revenues and expenditures for a fiscal year as approved by the Board of County Commissioners."
- **Legally Adopted Budget**: the budget as formally adopted, which sets the legal spending ceiling by fund.
- **Proposed Budget**: the budget presented for public review and workshops before the Board takes its final adoption vote. On this site, the "FY 2027" figures are the proposed budget until adoption is complete.
- **Amendment**: "A change to an adopted budget, which may increase or decrease a fund total. The change must be approved by the Board of County Commissioners."
- **Actual**: the closed, historical record of what a fund or account truly collected or spent in a completed fiscal year, as distinct from what was budgeted.

See [How to Read the Budget](how-to-read-the-budget.md) for a full walkthrough of how Actual, Budget, and Proposed columns relate on this site's tables.

## Organizational Terms

- **Department**: "An organizational unit of the County responsible for carrying out a major governmental function."
- **Division**: "A basic organizational unit of the County, which is functionally unique in its service delivery." A department can contain multiple divisions.
- **Constitutional Officers**: the separately elected offices established by the Florida Constitution (Clerk of Courts & County Comptroller, Property Appraiser, Sheriff, Supervisor of Elections, Tax Collector) whose budgets are included in the County's budget but who operate independently. See [Constitutional Officers](constitutional-officers.md).
- **Program / Activity**: a program is a specific service or initiative a department carries out; an activity is a broader functional grouping used to roll up expenditures (e.g. Public Safety, Transportation) across departments for summary reporting. See [Expenditure Guide](expenditure-guide.md).
- **Object Code**: "An account to which an expense or expenditure is recorded in order to accumulate and categorize the various types of payments that are made by governments. Certain object codes are mandated by the State of Florida Uniform Accounting System."

## Fund Terms

- **Fund**: "A fiscal and accounting entity with a self-balancing set of accounts recording cash and other financial resources, together with all related liabilities and residual equities or balances, and changes therein, which are segregated for the purpose of carrying on specific activities or attaining certain objectives in accordance with special regulations, restrictions, or limitations of that fund."
- **General Fund**: "The main operating fund for Walton County, which is used to account for all financial resources, except those required to be accounted for in another fund. The majority of funding is from ad valorem taxes. The collection and disbursement of monies to the constitutional officers is accounted for in this fund."
- **Special Revenue Fund**: "A governmental accounting fund used to account for the proceeds of specific revenue sources that are legally restricted to expenditures for specified purposes."
- **Capital Projects Funds**: "Account for financial resources to be used for the acquisition or construction of major capital facilities (other than those financed by the proprietary funds)."
- **Proprietary Funds**: "Enterprise Funds or Internal Service Funds in which operating revenues result from exchange transactions associated with the principal activity of the fund." (Sometimes called Enterprise Funds when they operate similarly to a business, recovering costs through user charges.)
- **Fund Balance**: "A term used to express the equity (assets minus liabilities) of governmental funds and trust funds." This is the formal accounting term behind what is commonly called a fund's "reserves."
- **Restricted vs. unrestricted revenue**: restricted revenue is legally required to be spent on a specific purpose (e.g. gas taxes for transportation); unrestricted revenue, like most of the General Fund's ad valorem tax revenue, can support any lawful county purpose the Board approves.

See [Fund Structure](fund-structure.md) and [Fund Descriptions](fund-descriptions.md) for how these terms apply to Walton County's 20 actual funds.

## Reserves, Transfers, and Carry-Forward

- **Reserve for Contingencies**: "An amount set aside, consistent with statutory authority that can subsequently be appropriated to meet unexpected needs."
- **Unencumbered Balance**: the portion of an appropriation not yet spent or legally committed.
- **Encumbrance**: a commitment of budgeted funds for a specific future expenditure (such as a purchase order) before the actual expense is incurred.
- **Interfund Transfers**: "Budgeted amounts transferred from one governmental accounting fund to another for work or services provided. These represent a 'double counting' of expenditures. Therefore, these amounts are deducted from the total County operating budget to calculate the 'net' budget."
- **Carry Forward**: unspent, previously appropriated funds (often tied to a specific project or grant) re-budgeted into the following fiscal year rather than lapsing.

## Revenue and Tax Terms

- **Ad Valorem Taxes**: a tax levied on the assessed value (net of any exemptions) of real or personal property — commonly called "property tax."
- **Millage Rate**: "A rate expressed in thousands. As used with ad valorem (property) taxes, the rate expresses the dollars of tax per one thousand dollars of taxable values."
- **Taxable Valuation**: "The value used for computing the ad valorem taxes levied against property. The taxable value is the assessed value less any exemptions allowed by law. The most common exemption is the $25,000 homestead exemption allowed if the owner uses the property as the principal residence. There are also exemptions for disability, government owned and non-profit owned property."
- **Grants**: "A contribution by a government or other organization to support a particular function. Grants may be classified as either operational or capital, depending upon the grantee."
- **Impact Fees**: "Fees charged to land developers at the time of development for the cost of construction of facilities to serve the development site (roads, parks, etc.)."
- **Recurring vs. one-time (non-recurring) revenue**: recurring revenue (like property taxes) is expected every year and can support ongoing operations; one-time revenue (like a single grant award or asset sale) should generally fund one-time expenditures rather than ongoing costs, since it cannot be relied on to repeat.

See [Revenue Guide](revenue-guide.md), [Property Tax](property-tax.md), and [Tourist Development Tax](tourist-development-tax.md) for full explanations of each revenue source.

## Capital and Debt Terms

- **Capital Improvements**: "Physical assets, constructed or purchased, that have a minimum useful life of five years. These may include buildings, recreational facilities, road and drainage structures, water and wastewater structures, and equipment."
- **Capital Outlay**: "Fixed assets which have a value of $5,000 or more and have a useful economic lifetime of more than one year; or assets of any value if the nature of the item is such that it must be controlled for custody purposes as a fixed asset."
- **Capital Project**: "A project to acquire or improve an asset with an anticipated life exceeding one year and includes land acquisition, construction, repair or structural improvement of a facility, engineering and design for a facility, and acquisition or repair of equipment."
- **Debt Service**: "Payments of principal and interest to bondholders."

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What's the difference between a Department and a Division?**
A Department carries out a major governmental function; a Division is a smaller organizational unit within a department with a functionally unique service delivery role.

**What's the difference between Capital Outlay and Capital Improvements?**
Capital Outlay is an accounting classification for individual fixed assets meeting a dollar/useful-life threshold; Capital Improvements refers more broadly to physical infrastructure assets with at least a five-year useful life, often tracked through the [Capital Improvement Plan](capital-projects.md).

**Why do Interfund Transfers get subtracted from the total budget?**
Because the same dollar would otherwise be counted once as an expenditure in the sending fund and again as revenue in the receiving fund — netting them out avoids double-counting the County's true total spending.

## Common Misconceptions

- **"Reserve for Contingencies is unused, wasted money."** It is a statutorily authorized safeguard for unexpected needs, not idle funds — see [Reserves](reserves.md).
- **"An Amendment means the original budget was wrong."** Amendments are a normal, legally provided mechanism for handling new information (grants, emergencies, carry-forward) during the fiscal year — see [Budget Process](budget-process.md).
