# Walton County Budget Process

## Purpose

This document explains how Walton County prepares, reviews, adopts, and amends its annual budget, and the Florida statutory requirements that govern the process.

## Overview

The annual budget process turns department needs, revenue forecasts, public priorities, Board direction, and Florida statutory requirements into an adopted spending plan for the fiscal year (October 1 – September 30). Walton County's budget moves through four phases before adoption, and remains an active financial management tool throughout the fiscal year afterward.

## The Four Phases

### Phase 1: Preparation

Budget development begins when departments receive instructions from the Office of Management and Budget (OMB). Departments prepare operating and capital requests based on service needs, staffing, projects, and funding assumptions, reviewing current programs, service demands, staffing, capital needs, and operating requirements before submitting requests.

### Phase 2: Review

OMB and County Administration evaluate department requests against revenue estimates, County priorities, fund capacity, and policy guidance, comparing proposed expenditures to available resources and preparing recommendations for Board consideration.

### Phase 3: Workshops

The tentative budget is reviewed publicly with the Board of County Commissioners, including funding requests, service levels, and the proposed millage (property tax) rate.

### Phase 4: Adoption

Florida law requires public hearings before the Board adopts the final millage rate and annual operating budget. This is the point at which the "proposed" budget becomes the "adopted" budget for the fiscal year. See [How to Read the Budget](how-to-read-the-budget.md) for the distinction between these terms.

## Basis of Budgeting

Walton County prepares and adopts its annual budget in accordance with Generally Accepted Accounting Principles (GAAP) and applicable Florida Statutes governing local government budgeting.

- **Budgeting standards**: annual budgets for governmental fund types legally required to prepare a budget are adopted on a basis consistent with GAAP.
- **Encumbrance accounting**: encumbrances represent commitments for goods or services, including purchase orders and contractual obligations, tracked so that money already committed isn't counted as available twice.
- **Modified accrual basis**: used for budgeting purposes and audited financial statements for governmental funds — revenue is recognized when it becomes available and measurable, and expenditures when the related liability is incurred, rather than strictly on a cash basis.
- **Grant revenue treatment**: grant revenue is generally added to the budget after grant agreements are executed, or prior-year grant balances are carried forward into the new fiscal year.

## Budget Implementation and Amendments

After adoption, the budget remains an active financial management tool. OMB and County departments monitor spending, revenues, appropriations, and amendments throughout the fiscal year. **Budgetary control is established at the fund level** for all funds, meaning total appropriations for each fund cannot legally exceed the amount adopted, even though individual line items within a fund can shift.

When circumstances require changes to the approved budget, the Board of County Commissioners may amend the budget by resolution in accordance with Section 129.06, Florida Statutes. Amendments may be needed for:

- Unforeseen expenditures
- Use of contingency reserves
- Allocation of unanticipated revenues
- Grant revenues received after adoption
- Carry-forward funding from the prior fiscal year

## Frequently Asked Questions

**When does Walton County's fiscal year run?**
October 1 through September 30, consistent with Florida's standard local government fiscal year.

**How and when is the budget prepared?**
Departments submit requests to OMB, which reviews them against revenue forecasts and Board priorities before public workshops and legally required public hearings, culminating in adoption of a final millage rate and budget. See the four phases above.

**Can the budget change after it's adopted?**
Yes. The Board may amend the budget by resolution under Section 129.06, Florida Statutes, for reasons like unanticipated revenue, grants, or carry-forward funds — but total appropriations for each fund must stay within legally adopted limits unless formally amended.

**What is a public hearing, and why does Florida law require one for the budget?**
A public hearing is a formally noticed meeting where residents can comment before the Board takes a final vote — required under Florida's Truth in Millage (TRIM) law before adopting a final millage rate and budget, ensuring transparency in how property tax rates are set.

## Common Misconceptions

- **"The budget is finalized the moment it's proposed."** A proposed budget still has to go through public review, workshops, and legally required hearings before it is adopted — see [How to Read the Budget](how-to-read-the-budget.md).
- **"Once adopted, the budget can never change."** It can be amended by Board resolution during the fiscal year under specific statutory conditions, though total fund-level appropriations remain legally controlled.
- **"Grant funding is available to spend as soon as a grant is announced."** Grant revenue is generally added to the budget only after the grant agreement is executed.

## Definitions

- **Millage rate**: the tax rate applied to a property's taxable value to calculate ad valorem (property) tax; one mill equals $1 of tax per $1,000 of taxable value.
- **TRIM (Truth in Millage)**: Florida's statutory process requiring truth-in-millage notices and public hearings before a taxing authority adopts a final millage rate.
- **Encumbrance**: a commitment of budgeted funds for a specific future expenditure, such as a purchase order, before the actual expense is incurred.
- **Budgetary control**: the legal requirement that spending not exceed the total appropriation adopted for each fund.
