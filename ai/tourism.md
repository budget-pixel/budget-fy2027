# Tourism

## Purpose

This document explains Walton County's tourism-related services and how they are funded.

## Overview

Walton County's coastal geography, including 26 miles of Gulf coastline and the Scenic Highway 30A/South Walton area, makes tourism a major economic driver. Tourism-related County services are funded primarily through the [Tourist Development Tax](tourist-development-tax.md), a tax on short-term lodging paid by visitors, legally restricted under Florida Statute 125.0104 to tourism-related purposes.

## Tourism Departments

- **Tourism Administration**: administers Tourist Development Tax-funded projects, focused on a stable, strong tourism revenue base and managing visitation consistently across the year.
- **Beach Operations**: day-to-day beach services and maintenance.
- **Beach Renourishment**: shoreline restoration and sand replenishment projects.
- **Marketing**: tourism promotion and advertising.
- **Sales and Visitors Center**: visitor information and hospitality services.
- **Communications**: tourism-related public communications.
- **Beach Tram**: visitor transportation services along the coast.
- **Tourism Lifeguard Services and Beach Safety**: beach lifeguard operations, a tourism-impact-related public safety service.
- **North Walton (Tourism)**: tourism development activities specific to the North Walton area, supported by its own dedicated portion of Tourist Development Tax collections.

## Why Tourism Funding Is Kept Separate

Because the Tourist Development Tax is legally restricted to tourism-related purposes, its revenue is accounted for in the dedicated Tourist Development Fund, separate from the General Fund — meaning it cannot be redirected to unrelated general government functions. See [Why Tourist Tax Is Restricted](intents/why-tourist-tax-is-restricted.md).

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does tourism funding pay for county-wide law enforcement?** Only where a specific cost is directly tied to tourism impact (e.g. beach lifeguard services) — not general county-wide patrol.

**Who pays for tourism promotion?** Visitors, through the Tourist Development Tax on short-term lodging — not county residents' property tax bills.

**Does every part of the county receive the same tourism funding treatment?** North Walton has its own dedicated tourism development funding stream, distinct from the broader county Tourist Development Fund allocation.

## Common Misconceptions

- **"Tourism dollars could fix any county problem if the Board chose to."** Tourist Development Tax revenue is legally restricted by state statute, not merely by County policy preference.
- **"Tourism only benefits visitors."** Tourism-funded beach management, marketing, and public safety services also directly serve residents who live in or near tourist areas.
