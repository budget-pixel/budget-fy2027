# Walton County Profile

## Purpose

This document provides factual background on Walton County, Florida — geography, history, and government structure — to give budget figures proper context.

## Overview

Walton County is located in northwest Florida, bordered by rivers, forests, lakes, and the Gulf of Mexico. Established in 1824, the county grew from early timber and agricultural communities into a diverse coastal and inland economy.

Walton County includes both inland communities rooted in agriculture, history, and small-town civic life, and coastal communities along Scenic Highway 30A and South Walton shaped by tourism, conservation, and recreation. This dual character — a large, historically rural inland area and a nationally known coastal tourist destination — directly shapes the County's budget: it is why the Tourist Development Tax funds a substantial share of coastal services (beach management, tourism marketing, lifeguard services) while ad valorem (property) taxes and gas taxes fund countywide services like law enforcement, roads, and general government.

### Geography

- 26 miles of Gulf of Mexico coastline
- Coastal dune lakes, a globally rare ecosystem concentrated along Walton County's coast
- Britton Hill, the highest natural point in the State of Florida, located in the county's inland area
- Forests, wetlands, rivers, and lakes across the inland portion of the county

### History

Walton County's history includes Native American communities, early settlement, the timber and lumber industries, and the Chautauqua educational tradition centered in DeFuniak Springs, the county seat.

## Government Structure

Walton County operates under a **commission-administrator form of government**:

- The **Board of County Commissioners (BCC)** — five members, one per district, elected at-large by all county voters — sets policy direction, enacts ordinances and resolutions, levies taxes and fees, adopts the annual budget, and appoints the County Administrator, Chief Financial Officer, and County Attorney.
- The **County Administrator** oversees day-to-day County operations and implements Board policy.
- Five offices are **separately elected Constitutional Officers** under the Florida Constitution, each operating independently of the Board once its budget is appropriated: Clerk of Courts & County Comptroller, Property Appraiser, Sheriff, Supervisor of Elections, and Tax Collector. See [Constitutional Officers](constitutional-officers.md).

Commissioners serve four-year terms. Districts One, Three, and Five are elected during presidential election years; Districts Two and Four are elected during midterm election years.

## Why This Matters for the Budget

- A coastal, tourism-dependent economy means the Tourist Development Tax is one of the County's largest revenue sources — but it is legally restricted to tourism-related uses and cannot fund general government operations. See [Tourist Development Tax](revenue-guide.md).
- A large unincorporated inland area means the County (rather than municipalities) is the primary service provider for most residents outside incorporated cities.
- Coastal geography drives significant capital investment in beach management, stormwater, and infrastructure resilience.
- The commission-administrator structure means budget policy decisions are made by elected commissioners, while day-to-day budget execution is managed by professional staff under the County Administrator and Office of Management and Budget.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many Commissioners does Walton County have, and how are they elected?**
Five, elected at-large by all county voters, each representing and residing in one of five geographic districts.

**Is Walton County's economy mostly tourism?**
Tourism is a major economic driver, particularly along the South Walton coast, but the county also has a substantial inland agricultural, timber-heritage, and residential economy. This split is reflected in the budget's mix of tourism-restricted revenue and general countywide revenue.

**What is a coastal dune lake, and why does it matter to the budget?**
Coastal dune lakes are a rare freshwater lake formation found in only a few places in the world, several of which are in Walton County. Their protection informs the Environmental Resources department's work and beach management spending funded through the Tourist Development Tax. See [Departments Overview](departments.md) (Environmental Resources) and [Tourism](tourism.md) (Beach Operations, Beach Renourishment).

## Common Misconceptions

- **"Walton County is just its beaches."** The county includes a large inland area with its own distinct economy, history, and service needs, funded largely through different revenue sources than the coastal tourism economy.
- **"The County Administrator runs everything, including the Sheriff's Office."** The County Administrator oversees Board-governed departments only. Constitutional Officers, including the Sheriff, operate independently. See [Constitutional Officers](constitutional-officers.md).

## Definitions

- **Unincorporated area**: land within the county not part of an incorporated city or town; the County is typically the primary local government service provider there.
- **DeFuniak Springs**: the county seat of Walton County.
