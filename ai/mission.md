# Mission and Purpose of Walton County Government

## Purpose

This document explains why Walton County government exists, what it is legally obligated to do, and how the annual budget connects to that purpose.

## Overview

Walton County is a political subdivision of the State of Florida. It exists to provide public services that either (a) state law requires a county to provide, (b) residents and the Board of County Commissioners have chosen to fund locally because no other level of government provides them, or (c) are more efficiently delivered at the county level than by individual municipalities or residents.

The County operates under a **commission-administrator form of government**: the five-member Board of County Commissioners (BCC) sets policy direction, enacts ordinances and resolutions, and adopts the annual budget, while a County Administrator — appointed by the Board — oversees day-to-day operations and implementation. The Board also appoints the Chief Financial Officer and County Attorney.

Each commissioner represents and resides in one of five geographic districts but is elected at-large by all county voters. Districts One, Three, and Five are elected during presidential election years; Districts Two and Four are elected during midterm election years.

In addition to the Board-governed departments, five offices are **separately elected Constitutional Officers** under the Florida Constitution: the Clerk of Courts & County Comptroller, Property Appraiser, Sheriff, Supervisor of Elections, and Tax Collector (see [Constitutional Officers](constitutional-officers.md) for the full explanation of why this distinction matters). Each Constitutional Officer's budget is included in the County's annual budget for appropriation purposes, but each office sets its own internal policies, hiring, and line-item budget management independently.

## Why the Budget Exists

The annual budget is the legal instrument through which the Board of County Commissioners authorizes County government to collect revenue and spend public funds. Under Florida Statutes (primarily Chapters 129 and 200), the County is required to:

- Adopt a balanced budget for each fiscal year (October 1 – September 30)
- Hold public hearings before adopting a final millage (property tax) rate and budget
- Establish budgetary control at the fund level, so no fund's total appropriations exceed what was legally adopted
- Publish Truth-in-Millage (TRIM) notices so property owners can see proposed tax changes before they take effect

See [Budget Process](budget-process.md) for the full annual cycle and [Financial Policies](financial-policies.md) for the adopted policies governing budget preparation and amendment.

## Why Services Exist the Way They Do

Every dollar in the budget funds a specific public service, and most services exist for one of these reasons:

- **State mandate**: some services (e.g. property appraisal, tax collection, elections administration, court support) are required by Florida law and are typically the responsibility of a Constitutional Officer rather than a Board department.
- **Public safety and health**: law enforcement, fire rescue, emergency management, and public health services protect residents and visitors and are core functions nearly every Florida county provides.
- **Infrastructure and growth management**: roads, drainage, planning, permitting, and capital projects exist because the County is responsible for public infrastructure that private development alone would not adequately provide or maintain.
- **Quality of life and economic vitality**: parks, libraries, recreation, and tourism-related services (funded largely by the Tourist Development Tax, not property taxes — see [Revenue Guide](revenue-guide.md)) support residents' quality of life and the visitor economy that is central to Walton County.
- **Environmental stewardship**: beach management, environmental resources, mosquito control, and solid waste services protect natural resources and public health in a county with 26 miles of Gulf coastline and significant coastal dune lake ecosystems.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why does the County provide services instead of the state or a city?**
Florida counties are the primary unit of local government in unincorporated areas and provide many services statewide, either by statutory mandate or because residents in unincorporated areas have no municipal government to provide them.

**Does the Board of County Commissioners control every department's budget?**
The Board adopts the overall budget and appropriates funds, but Constitutional Officers manage their own offices' internal operations and line-item spending independently once their budget is approved. See [Constitutional Officers](constitutional-officers.md).

**Is the budget the same as the County's mission statement?**
The budget is the financial expression of the County's obligations and priorities for one fiscal year. It is shaped by legal mandates, Board policy direction, and resident priorities, but it is not itself a mission statement.

## Common Misconceptions

- **"The County can spend money on whatever it wants."** Many funds are legally restricted to specific purposes (e.g. gas tax revenue for transportation, tourist development tax for tourism-related uses). See [Fund Structure](fund-structure.md) and [Revenue Guide](revenue-guide.md).
- **"The Sheriff, Property Appraiser, and other Constitutional Officers work for the County Administrator."** They do not. They are independently elected and legally distinct from the Board-governed County departments, even though their budgets appear in the same document.

## Definitions

- **Board of County Commissioners (BCC)**: the five-member elected legislative and policy-making body of Walton County government.
- **County Administrator**: appointed by the Board to oversee day-to-day County operations.
- **Constitutional Officer**: an office established directly by the Florida Constitution and filled by separate election, operating independently of the Board once its budget is set.
