# Impact Fees

## Purpose

This document explains what impact fees are, who pays them, and what they can legally fund.

## Overview

Impact fees are "fees charged to land developers at the time of development for the cost of construction of facilities to serve the development site (roads, parks, etc.)." Unlike a tax, an impact fee exists specifically so that new development pays for the infrastructure burden its own growth creates, rather than spreading that cost across existing taxpayers.

## The Rational Nexus Requirement

Florida impact fee law requires a "rational nexus" — a clear, legally defensible connection — between the fee charged and the specific type of infrastructure it funds. A transportation impact fee can only fund transportation infrastructure; a parks impact fee can only fund parks infrastructure. These categories cannot be commingled with each other or spent on unrelated general government purposes.

## Related Mechanisms in Walton County

- **Mobility Fee Fund**: fees supporting multi-modal transportation infrastructure tied to new development, implemented through the County's Mobility Plan.
- **Recreation Plat Fee Fund**: developer assessments supporting recreational facilities.

These function similarly to traditional impact fees — charging new development for the specific category of infrastructure it requires.

## How Impact Fees Differ From Special Assessments (MSBUs)

An impact fee is a one-time charge tied to new development's own infrastructure burden. A Municipal Service Benefit Unit (MSBU) special assessment funds a specific local improvement benefiting a defined existing geographic area, independent of new growth. Both are "special assessments" in the broad glossary sense — "compulsory levies against certain properties to defray all or part of the cost of specific improvements or services presumed to be of general benefit to the public and of special benefit to the assessed property" — but they serve different triggering purposes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do existing residents pay impact fees?** No — impact fees are charged to developers at the time of new development, not assessed on existing property owners' regular tax bills.

**Can impact fee revenue fund a project unrelated to new growth?** Generally no — it must fund infrastructure connected to the growth impact that generated the fee, under Florida's rational nexus requirement.

**What's the difference between the Mobility Fee and a traditional transportation impact fee?** Both charge new development for transportation infrastructure it requires; the Mobility Fee Fund in Walton County implements this specifically through the county's Mobility Plan framework.

**Does Walton County have a parks impact fee?** The Recreation Plat Fee Fund performs an equivalent function, funding recreational facilities through developer assessments.

## Common Misconceptions

- **"Impact fees can be spent anywhere the County needs money."** They are restricted to the specific infrastructure category they were charged for.
- **"Impact fees are just another property tax."** They are one-time development charges, not recurring taxes on existing property ownership.
