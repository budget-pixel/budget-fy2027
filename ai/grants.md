# Grants

## Purpose

This document explains how Walton County receives, budgets, and administers grant funding.

## Overview

A grant is "a contribution by a government or other organization to support a particular function. Grants may be classified as either operational or capital, depending upon the grantee." Grants are a component of Intergovernmental Revenues on the revenue side and Grants and Aid on the expenditure side (where the County itself distributes funding to other organizations, such as the Non-Profit Funding Program). See [Revenue Guide](revenue-guide.md) and [Expenditure Guide](expenditure-guide.md).

## How Grant Revenue Is Budgeted

Grant revenue is generally added to the budget only after the underlying grant agreement is executed, or as prior-year grant balances are carried forward into the new fiscal year — not simply when a grant is announced or applied for. This means a department's budget may grow mid-year as new grants are formally awarded, distinct from routine budget amendments for other reasons.

## Grants Administration

The County's Grants Administration policy provides guidance for the administration, monitoring, and reporting of grant-funded activities, ensuring compliance with each grant's specific requirements and maximizing effective use of external funding. This includes compliance with grantor reporting deadlines, allowable-cost rules, and — for many grants — a required local funding match.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is grant money "free" money with no strings attached?** No — grants come with specific program requirements, reporting obligations, and often a required local match; misuse can require repayment to the grantor.

**When does a grant show up in the budget?** Generally only after the grant agreement is executed, not when it is merely announced or applied for.

**Does the County give grants to other organizations, or only receive them?** Both — the County receives grants (Intergovernmental Revenue) and also distributes funding to nonprofits and other agencies (Grants and Aid), such as through the Non-Profit Funding Program.

**Who oversees grant compliance?** Grant administration follows the County's adopted Grants Administration policy; see Financial Policies.

## Common Misconceptions

- **"A grant is guaranteed, ongoing revenue."** Most grants are one-time or time-limited and cannot be relied on to repeat, unlike recurring revenue such as property taxes.
- **"Grant money can be spent on anything.** Grant funds are restricted to the specific program or project defined in the grant agreement.
