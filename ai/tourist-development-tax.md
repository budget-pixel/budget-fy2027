# Tourist Development Tax

## Purpose

This document explains what the Tourist Development Tax is, who pays it, and what it can legally fund in Walton County.

## Overview

"The local government Tourist Development Tax was established by Florida Statute 125.0104 in 1977 to provide counties with an option to raise additional revenue for tourism related projects or activities." It is a local option tax on short-term lodging (hotels, vacation rentals, and similar accommodations), paid by visitors rather than assessed on county residents' property.

Because the tax's authorizing statute defines a specific purpose, Walton County deposits the revenue into the dedicated **Tourist Development Fund** (Fund 111), one of the county's six major governmental funds, rather than the General Fund. See [Fund Descriptions](fund-descriptions.md).

## What It Funds

Tourist Development Tax revenue supports:

- Tourism promotion and marketing
- Beach renourishment and beach management
- Visitor-serving infrastructure
- Tourism-related public safety initiatives (such as beach lifeguard services)

Departments and programs primarily funded through this revenue include Tourism Administration, Beach Operations, Beach Renourishment, Marketing, the Sales and Visitors Center, and Tourism Lifeguard Services and Beach Safety. See [Tourism](tourism.md) for detail on each.

## What It Cannot Fund

Because the tax is legally restricted to tourism-related purposes under Section 125.0104, Florida Statutes, it cannot be redirected to unrelated general government functions — countywide law enforcement patrol, general administration, or the court system, for example — unless a specific expenditure meets the statutory tourism-related nexus. See [Why Tourist Tax Is Restricted](intents/why-tourist-tax-is-restricted.md) for a full explanation.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Who actually pays the Tourist Development Tax?** Visitors renting short-term lodging — it is not part of a resident's regular property tax bill.

**Why can't the County use this tax for road repairs unrelated to tourism?** The tax's authorizing statute restricts its use to tourism-related purposes; using it for an unrelated purpose would violate state law, not just County policy.

**Does every Florida county charge this tax?** It is a local option available under state law; Walton County has elected to levy it.

**Does the Tourist Development Fund pay for the Sheriff's Office?** Only where a specific public safety cost is directly tied to tourism impact (such as beach lifeguard services) — not for general county-wide law enforcement operations, which are funded through the Sheriff Fund and General Fund.

## Common Misconceptions

- **"Tourist tax money is just sitting unused."** It funds an active, ongoing set of tourism promotion, beach management, and visitor-serving infrastructure programs — its restriction to those purposes is what sometimes makes it appear separate from other pressing needs.
- **"Residents pay the Tourist Development Tax on their property."** It is a tax on short-term lodging rentals paid by visitors, not a property tax.
