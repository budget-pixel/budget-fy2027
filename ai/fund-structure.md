# Fund Structure

## Purpose

This document explains why Walton County organizes its finances into separate funds, and how those funds are categorized. See [Fund Descriptions](fund-descriptions.md) for what each individual fund is for.

## Overview

Walton County uses **fund accounting** to separate resources by legal restriction, program purpose, and intended use. This structure supports transparency, budgetary control, and clear reporting for operating services, capital investments, business-type activities, and resources held on behalf of others.

A **fund** is a fiscal and accounting entity with a self-balancing set of accounts recording cash and other financial resources, together with related liabilities and residual equities, segregated for a specific activity or objective under specific regulations, restrictions, or limitations. In plain terms: money that comes in for a restricted purpose (like a gas tax that can only fund roads) is kept in its own fund, tracked separately from unrestricted General Fund revenue, so it can never be accidentally spent on something it wasn't legally raised to support.

Walton County has **20 funds** in total, falling into three categories:

### Governmental Funds

Used to account for core public services, including general operations, transportation, public safety, tourism, capital projects, grants, assessments, and other restricted activities. Walton County's governmental funds include:

**Major Governmental Funds** (the largest, most significant funds, reported individually):
- General Fund (001)
- Transportation Fund (101)
- Sheriff Fund (107)
- Tourist Development Fund (111)
- Solid Waste Fund (112)
- Capital Projects Fund (300)

**Non-Major Governmental Funds** (smaller special-purpose funds, reported individually on fund schedules but grouped together on some summary tables):
- Debt Service Fund (201)
- Building Fund (103)
- E911 Fund (109)
- Housing & Urban Development Fund (110)
- Law Enforcement Trust Fund (108)
- Mobility Fee Fund (116)
- Mosquito Control Fund (105)
- Mosquito Control State Aid Fund (106)
- MSBU Fund (102)
- Preservation Fund (113)
- Recreation Plat Fee Fund (114)
- Sidewalk Fund (115)
- State Housing Initiative Program (SHIP) Fund (104)

### Proprietary Funds (Business-Type)

Used for activities that operate similarly to business-type functions or internal service activities, where costs are recovered through user charges or internal cost allocations rather than taxes. Walton County's proprietary fund:
- Self-Insurance Fund (503) — an Internal Service Fund

### Fiduciary Funds (Custodial)

Used to account for resources held by the County on behalf of other individuals, agencies, governments, or funds. (Walton County's fiduciary activity, where applicable, is reported separately from the governmental and proprietary funds listed above.)

## Why This Structure Matters

- **Legal compliance**: many revenue sources (gas taxes, tourist development taxes, impact fees, special assessments) are restricted by Florida law or local ordinance to specific uses. Fund accounting is how the County proves, transparently, that restricted money is actually spent on its restricted purpose.
- **Budgetary control**: budgetary control is established at the fund level — total appropriations in each fund cannot legally exceed what the Board adopted for that fund, regardless of how individual line items shift. See [Budget Process](budget-process.md).
- **Clarity for residents**: separating funds lets a resident see exactly what their Tourist Development Tax dollars fund, separately from their property tax dollars, rather than one undifferentiated pool of money.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why does Walton County have 20 different funds instead of one big budget?**
Because much of the County's revenue is legally restricted to specific purposes. Fund accounting is the mechanism that keeps restricted revenue from being spent on unrelated purposes and lets the County demonstrate compliance.

**What's the difference between a Major and Non-Major fund?**
It's a reporting distinction based on relative size and significance, not a difference in legal restriction — a Non-Major fund's revenue can be just as legally restricted as a Major fund's.

**Can money move between funds?**
Yes, through Interfund Transfers, but only for legitimate purposes (like the General Fund subsidizing a service in another fund) — see [Budget Terminology](budget-terminology.md) for how transfers are counted to avoid double-counting the County's total budget.

**Does every fund get its own tax or fee?**
Most special revenue funds are tied to a specific revenue source (e.g. the Tourist Development Fund to the Tourist Development Tax), but some funds receive transfers from other funds in addition to or instead of a dedicated revenue source.

## Common Misconceptions

- **"All County money is one pot the Board can spend on anything."** Most non-General-Fund revenue is legally restricted to the purpose of its own fund — see [Fund Descriptions](fund-descriptions.md) and [Revenue Guide](revenue-guide.md).
- **"A fund with a large balance has 'extra' money to spend freely."** A fund balance may be legally restricted, committed to a specific purpose, or held as a reserve required by policy — see [Reserves](reserves.md).
- **"Major and Non-Major means more or less important."** It is a financial reporting size threshold, not a statement about a service's importance.
