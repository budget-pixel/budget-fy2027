# Constitutional Officers

## Purpose

This document explains what a Constitutional Officer is, why Walton County's five Constitutional Officers are treated differently from Board-governed departments, and what each office does.

## Overview

Florida's Constitution establishes five county offices that are filled by separate, direct election rather than appointment: the **Clerk of Courts & County Comptroller, Property Appraiser, Sheriff, Supervisor of Elections, and Tax Collector**. Each is a **Constitutional Officer** — legally independent of the Board of County Commissioners (BCC) and the County Administrator, even though each office's budget is included in the County's overall annual budget for appropriation purposes.

Once the Board approves a Constitutional Officer's budget, that office "operates as a separate entity, setting its own policies and procedures, employee hiring and line-item budget management" — a phrase that appears, in substance, in each office's own official narrative. The Board does not direct how a Constitutional Officer spends their appropriated budget line by line, hires staff, or sets internal office procedures.

## The Five Constitutional Officers

### Clerk of Courts & County Comptroller
A separately elected Constitutional Officer who also acts as Clerk to the Board of County Commissioners. Supports judicial functions through the Civil Division, Family Services, Felony Division, Jury Administration, Juvenile and Recording Divisions. As Clerk to the Board, also provides Finance, Information Systems, and BCC recording functions — including accounting and auditing services, contract and purchase disbursements, investment of Board funds, information systems support for court and accounting services, and maintaining the County's "Official Records." The Clerk's budget is included in the County's General Fund for the portion of operations not covered by service fees.

### Property Appraiser
A separately elected Constitutional Officer whose budget is approved by the Florida Department of Revenue. Florida law requires the Board of County Commissioners to pay municipalities' and the school board's share of the Property Appraiser's budget cost. Responsible for determining the value of all property within the County, maintaining related records, calculating tax on taxable property once taxes are levied, and distributing Truth-in-Millage (TRIM) notices.

### Sheriff's Office
A separately elected Constitutional Officer whose functions include Corrections, Court Services, Law Enforcement, Animal Services, and Fire Rescue. The Corrections division operates the County detention facility (inmate housing, medical services, food services, prisoner transportation). Court Services provides bailiffs, courtroom security, and jury/judicial protective services. Law Enforcement includes uniformed road patrol, criminal investigations, crime scene investigation, K-9 units, and the E911 Communications Center. **The Walton County Sheriff's Office has also taken on the operation of the animal control facility and units, and the operations of Walton County Fire Rescue Services within the county** — meaning animal control, fire rescue, and 911 dispatch are all Sheriff's Office functions in Walton County, not separate agencies. See [Public Safety](public-safety.md).

### Supervisor of Elections
A separately elected Constitutional Officer whose overall budget is included in the General Fund. Administers elections and maintains voter registration records and statistics, ensuring compliance with the federally mandated National Voter Registration Act — including coordinating and training poll workers, setting up precinct polling places, ballot printing, and providing voting booths meeting minimum standards under Florida Statutes.

### Tax Collector
A separately elected Constitutional Officer whose budget is approved by the Florida Department of Revenue. Fee charges to the County's various taxing authorities are based on a legislated percentage of taxes collected for each authority. The Office's primary role is as an agent of state government: administering vehicle and vessel titling/registration as an agent of the Department of Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles, issuing hunting and fishing licenses as an agent for the Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission, collecting sales tax on vehicle and vessel sales as an agent for the Florida Department of Revenue, and collecting, investing, and distributing real and tangible property taxes (including delinquent tax collection) and occupational license tax as an agent of local and state government.

## Why This Matters for the Budget

- **Budget appropriation vs. budget control**: the Board decides how much total funding each Constitutional Officer's office receives, but does not control how that office spends it internally.
- **Different approval authorities**: the Property Appraiser's and Tax Collector's budgets are approved by the Florida Department of Revenue, not solely the Board — a state-level check unique to those offices.
- **Distinct legal responsibilities**: each office performs functions specifically assigned to it by Florida Statutes or the Florida Constitution, separate from the general governmental functions the Board and County Administrator oversee.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does the County Administrator supervise the Sheriff?**
No. The Sheriff is an independently elected Constitutional Officer, not an employee of the County Administrator or Board.

**Why are animal control, fire rescue, and 911 dispatch part of the Sheriff's Office budget rather than separate departments?**
In Walton County, the Sheriff's Office has taken on operational responsibility for the animal control facility/units, Walton County Fire Rescue Services, and the E911 Communications Center, in addition to its traditional law enforcement, corrections, and court services functions.

**Who approves the Property Appraiser's and Tax Collector's budgets?**
The Florida Department of Revenue approves both, reflecting the state-level regulatory role those two offices play in the property tax system.

**Can the Board cut a Constitutional Officer's budget?**
The Board approves the total appropriation for each Constitutional Officer during the annual budget process, but Florida law provides specific procedures and protections for how those budgets are set and disputed, differing from how a Board-governed department's budget is set.

## Common Misconceptions

- **"Constitutional Officers are County departments."** They are independently elected offices operating under their own authority once budgeted — see [Mission and Purpose](mission.md).
- **"The Sheriff's Office only does law enforcement."** In Walton County it also runs corrections, court security, animal control, fire rescue, and 911 dispatch.
- **"The Board can tell the Sheriff how to spend the Sheriff Fund."** The Board appropriates the total; the Sheriff's Office manages its own line-item spending, hiring, and internal policy within that appropriation.

## Definitions

- **Constitutional Officer**: an office established directly by the Florida Constitution and filled by separate election, operating independently of the Board of County Commissioners once its budget is set.
