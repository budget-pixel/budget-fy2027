# Intent: How does growth affect Walton County's budget?

## Question

How does population and development growth affect Walton County's budget and spending?

## Short Answer

Growth increases the demand for infrastructure and services (roads, permitting, public safety, water/sewer capacity), which the County manages partly through growth-pays-for-growth mechanisms like impact fees and mobility fees, and partly through long-range planning tools like the Comprehensive Plan, Land Development Code, and concurrency management, which the Planning Department administers.

## Detailed Explanation

The Walton County Planning & Development Services Department provides professional land use planning advice to the Board of County Commissioners and is responsible for implementing, maintaining, and updating the Comprehensive Plan and Land Development Code, concurrency management (including implementation of the Mobility Plan), floodplain management, and development review in unincorporated Walton County — ensuring proposed development complies with adopted codes, policies, and regulations.

**Concurrency management** is the growth-management principle that public infrastructure and services (roads, water/sewer, schools, parks) must be available concurrent with the impacts of new development — meaning growth cannot outpace the infrastructure needed to support it. Impact fees and the Mobility Fee Fund operationalize part of this principle financially, requiring new development to fund the specific infrastructure its own growth demands (see How Impact Fees Work).

Beyond fee-funded infrastructure, growth increases demand across nearly every County service: more permitting and code compliance activity, more calls for law enforcement and emergency services, more demand on parks and libraries, and more pressure on water/wastewater systems like the Mossy Head Wastewater Treatment Facility. Some of this added service demand is funded through growth-linked fees; some is funded through general recurring revenue growth (since new development also adds to the property tax base over time) and requires ongoing budget planning rather than one-time fee revenue.

## Why This Matters

Understanding growth's dual effect — new revenue from impact fees and an expanded tax base, alongside new demand for services and infrastructure — clarifies why a growing county's budget both grows and requires careful long-range planning, rather than automatically becoming easier to fund.

## Legal Context

Florida's growth management framework requires local comprehensive plans, land development regulations, and concurrency management consistent with state statutes; impact fees must satisfy the "rational nexus" test under Florida impact fee law.

## Frequently Asked Follow-Up Questions

**Does new development pay for its own infrastructure needs?** Substantially, through impact fees and the Mobility Fee Fund, under Florida's rational nexus requirement, though not every growth-related cost is fully offset by fees.

**What is concurrency management?** The requirement that necessary public infrastructure and services be available concurrent with new development's impacts, rather than allowing growth to outpace infrastructure capacity.

**Does growth increase property tax revenue automatically?** Growth generally expands the taxable value base over time, which can increase ad valorem revenue even without a millage rate increase — but new development also increases service demand and costs.

**Which department manages growth review?** The Planning Department reviews development proposals for compliance with the Comprehensive Plan, Land Development Code, and concurrency requirements.

## Key Takeaways

- Growth increases both revenue opportunity (impact fees, expanded tax base) and service/infrastructure demand.
- Concurrency management requires infrastructure capacity to keep pace with development impacts.
- Impact fees and the Mobility Fee Fund apply the "growth pays for growth" principle to specific infrastructure categories.
- The Planning Department administers the Comprehensive Plan, Land Development Code, and development review that manage this balance.
