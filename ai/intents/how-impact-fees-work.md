# Intent: How do impact fees work in Walton County?

## Question

What are impact fees, who pays them, and what can they fund?

## Short Answer

Impact fees are one-time charges paid by land developers at the time of development to cover the cost of constructing infrastructure (roads, parks, and similar facilities) needed to serve that new development — and Florida law requires a demonstrated "rational nexus" connecting the fee to the specific infrastructure burden the development creates, so impact fee revenue can only be spent on that matching category of infrastructure.

## Detailed Explanation

Impact fees are fees charged to land developers at the time of development for the cost of constructing facilities to serve the development site, such as roads and parks. Unlike a tax, an impact fee is directly tied to new growth: it exists specifically so that the infrastructure cost created by new development is paid by that development, rather than spread across all existing taxpayers who did not create the added demand.

Florida impact fee law requires a "rational nexus" — a clear, defensible connection — between the fee charged and the specific type of infrastructure it funds. This means a transportation impact fee can only fund transportation infrastructure, and a parks impact fee can only fund parks infrastructure; the categories cannot be commingled with each other or with unrelated general government spending.

In Walton County, related mechanisms include the Mobility Fee Fund (fees supporting multi-modal transportation infrastructure tied to new development) and the Recreation Plat Fee Fund (developer assessments supporting recreational facilities).

## Why This Matters

Understanding impact fees explains why a rapidly growing area may see new roads or parks built alongside development, funded by that development itself, while a resident's own property tax bill is not the source of that specific infrastructure spending.

## Legal Context

Florida impact fee law (developed through state statute and case law) requires the "rational nexus" test connecting a fee to the specific infrastructure need it funds, and generally requires fees to be proportionate to the burden new development creates.

## Budget Context

Impact fee and mobility fee revenue appears under Permits, Fees, and Special Assessments on the Summary of Revenues, and funds transportation and recreation capital projects in the relevant funds.

## Frequently Asked Follow-Up Questions

**Can impact fee revenue be used for a road project unrelated to new growth?** Generally no — it must fund infrastructure connected to the growth impact that generated the fee.

**Do existing residents pay impact fees?** No — impact fees are charged to developers at the time of new development, not to existing property owners on their regular tax bills.

**What's the difference between an impact fee and a special assessment (MSBU)?** An impact fee is tied to new development's infrastructure burden; a special assessment (MSBU) funds a specific local improvement benefiting a defined existing area, regardless of new growth.

**Does Walton County have a Mobility Fee in addition to traditional impact fees?** Yes — the Mobility Fee Fund supports multi-modal transportation infrastructure tied to new development.

- Impact fees are one-time developer charges tied specifically to the infrastructure burden new development creates.

- Walton County's Mobility Fee Fund and Recreation Plat Fee Fund apply this same principle to transportation and recreation infrastructure.
