# Intent: How is Walton County's budget created?

## Question

How does Walton County create its annual budget, from start to finish?

## Short Answer

The budget moves through four phases — Preparation, Review, Workshops, and Adoption — beginning around February and ending in September with the Board of County Commissioners' legal adoption of a final millage rate and budget, as required by Florida Statutes Chapters 125, 129, 200, and 218.

## Detailed Explanation

**Phase 1 — Preparation**: departments receive instructions from the Office of Management and Budget (OMB) and prepare operating and capital requests based on service needs, staffing, projects, and funding assumptions, reviewing current programs and requirements.

**Phase 2 — Review**: OMB and County Administration evaluate department requests against revenue estimates, County priorities, fund capacity, and policy guidance, comparing proposed expenditures to available resources and preparing recommendations for the Board.

**Phase 3 — Workshops**: the tentative budget is reviewed publicly with the Board, including funding requests, service levels, and the proposed millage rate.

**Phase 4 — Adoption**: Florida law requires public hearings before the Board adopts the final millage rate and annual operating budget.

The budget is prepared on a basis consistent with Generally Accepted Accounting Principles (GAAP) and the modified accrual basis for governmental funds, with encumbrance accounting used to track outstanding commitments and grant revenue generally added only after the underlying grant agreement is executed (or as prior-year grant balances carry forward). Budgetary control is established at the fund level, meaning total appropriations for each fund cannot legally exceed what the Board adopts, even as individual line items shift throughout the year.

After adoption, the budget remains an active management tool: OMB and departments monitor spending, revenue, and appropriations throughout the fiscal year, and the Board may amend the budget by resolution under Section 129.06, Florida Statutes, when unforeseen expenditures, contingency needs, unanticipated revenue, grants, or carry-forward funding require it.

## Why This Matters

Understanding the multi-month, multi-phase process — and that it culminates in legally required public hearings — clarifies both how much public input opportunity exists and why the budget can't be changed on short notice outside the formal amendment process.

## Legal Context

Chapters 125, 129, 200, and 218, Florida Statutes govern the county budget process broadly; Section 129.06 governs mid-year amendments; Florida's Truth in Millage (TRIM) law governs the public notice and hearing requirements before final millage adoption.

## Frequently Asked Follow-Up Questions

**When does the budget process start and end?** It begins around February and ends in September with legal adoption, for a fiscal year beginning October 1.

**Can the public comment on the budget before it's adopted?** Yes, at the required public hearings in the Adoption phase, and often during the public Workshops phase as well.

**What accounting basis does the County use?** The modified accrual basis for governmental funds, consistent with GAAP.

**Can the budget change after October 1?** Yes, by Board resolution under Section 129.06, Florida Statutes, for specific defined circumstances.

## Key Takeaways

- The budget process runs roughly February through September, in four phases: Preparation, Review, Workshops, Adoption.
- Florida law requires public hearings before the Board adopts a final millage rate and budget.
- The budget uses GAAP-consistent, modified accrual accounting, with fund-level budgetary control.
- The Board can amend the adopted budget by resolution under specific statutory conditions during the fiscal year.
