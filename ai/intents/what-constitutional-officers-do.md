# Intent: What do Walton County's Constitutional Officers do?

## Question

What are Walton County's Constitutional Officers, and what does each one do?

## Short Answer

Walton County has five separately elected Constitutional Officers under the Florida Constitution — the Clerk of Courts & County Comptroller, Property Appraiser, Sheriff, Supervisor of Elections, and Tax Collector — each legally independent of the Board of County Commissioners once its budget is appropriated, performing functions specifically assigned by Florida law.

## Detailed Explanation

- **Clerk of Courts & County Comptroller**: supports judicial functions (Civil Division, Family Services, Felony Division, Jury Administration, Juvenile and Recording Divisions) and, as Clerk to the Board, provides Finance, Information Systems, and BCC recording services, including accounting/auditing, contract and purchase disbursements, investment of Board funds, and maintaining the County's Official Records.
- **Property Appraiser**: determines the value of all property in the County, maintains related records, calculates tax on taxable property once levied, and distributes Truth-in-Millage (TRIM) notices; its budget is approved by the Florida Department of Revenue, and the Board must pay municipalities' and the school board's share of its cost.
- **Sheriff's Office**: law enforcement (patrol, investigations, K-9, crime scene investigation), corrections (detention facility operations), court services (bailiffs, courtroom security), and — specifically in Walton County — animal control and Walton County Fire Rescue Services, plus the E911 Communications Center.
- **Supervisor of Elections**: administers elections and maintains voter registration records, ensuring compliance with the federally mandated National Voter Registration Act, including poll worker coordination, precinct setup, ballot printing, and voting equipment.
- **Tax Collector**: acts primarily as an agent of state government — administering vehicle/vessel titling and registration (for the Department of Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles), issuing hunting/fishing licenses (for the Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission), collecting sales tax on vehicle/vessel sales (for the Department of Revenue), and collecting/investing/distributing real and tangible property taxes; its budget is also approved by the Florida Department of Revenue.

Each office, once its budget is approved by the Board (and, for the Property Appraiser and Tax Collector, the Florida Department of Revenue), "operates as a separate entity, setting its own policies and procedures, employee hiring, and line-item budget management" — meaning the Board does not direct day-to-day operations within an approved budget.

## Why This Matters

Residents often assume every County office reports to the County Administrator. Understanding which offices are independently elected clarifies who is actually accountable for a given service or decision.

## Legal Context

Established by the Florida Constitution; specific operational duties assigned by various Florida Statutes (e.g., Section 27.52 and 27.54 for Public Defender support, distinct from Constitutional Officers but a related state-mandated function; property tax and elections law for the Property Appraiser, Tax Collector, and Supervisor of Elections).

## Frequently Asked Follow-Up Questions

**Is the County Administrator the Sheriff's boss?** No — the Sheriff is independently elected and not supervised by the County Administrator or Board.

**Who approves the Property Appraiser's budget?** The Florida Department of Revenue, in addition to Board appropriation.

**Does the Clerk of Courts work for the courts or the County?** Both, in different capacities — as Clerk of the Court supporting judicial functions, and as Clerk to the Board of County Commissioners providing finance and recording services.

**Why does the Tax Collector issue hunting and fishing licenses?** As an agent of the Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission — one of several state-agent roles the office performs alongside its core tax collection duties.

## Key Takeaways

- Five Constitutional Officers serve Walton County: Clerk of Courts & County Comptroller, Property Appraiser, Sheriff, Supervisor of Elections, Tax Collector.
- Each is independently elected and operates its own office once its budget is approved.
- The Property Appraiser's and Tax Collector's budgets require Florida Department of Revenue approval in addition to Board appropriation.
- In Walton County specifically, the Sheriff's Office also runs animal control, fire rescue, and 911 dispatch.

This is a critical distinction from Walton County's Board-governed departments (like Public Works or Planning), which report through the County Administrator to the Board.
