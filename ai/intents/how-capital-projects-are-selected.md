# Intent: How are capital projects selected in Walton County?

## Question

How does Walton County decide which capital (infrastructure) projects get funded, and in what order?

## Short Answer

Capital projects are evaluated and planned through the multi-year Capital Improvement Plan (CIP), which weighs infrastructure and facility needs against available funding — current revenues, grants, and debt financing — and schedules projects across the years each is expected to take to design, permit, and build.

## Detailed Explanation

A capital improvement budget is a long-range plan for the construction, purchase, repair, or replacement of physical assets such as buildings, streets, parks, and drainage systems — distinct from the annual operating budget, which covers recurring costs like salaries and utilities. A capital project itself is defined as a project to acquire or improve an asset with an anticipated life exceeding one year, including land acquisition, construction, repair or structural improvement of a facility, engineering and design, and acquisition or repair of equipment; detailed information for each capital improvement includes its time frame for completion, location, description, estimated total expenditure, and proposed financing method.

Because many capital projects take longer than a single fiscal year to design, permit, and construct, they are budgeted across multiple years in the Capital Improvement Plan rather than forced entirely into one year's budget. Funding for capital projects typically comes through a combination of current revenues, grants, and debt financing, depending on the project's size, urgency, and the fund it relates to (for example, transportation projects draw on the Transportation Fund and related grant/impact fee revenue; Sheriff-related capital needs draw on the Sheriff Fund).

The County's Capital Projects Fund itself accounts for resources designated for acquiring, constructing, and improving major capital facilities and infrastructure, separate from day-to-day operating funds.

## Why This Matters

Understanding that capital projects are planned years in advance, and funded through a specific mix of sources, explains why a project a resident wants may be "on the list" for a future year rather than immediate, and why some projects move forward with grant or debt funding rather than solely current tax revenue.

## Legal Context

Capital improvement planning and disclosure requirements are informed by Florida growth management law (comprehensive planning, concurrency management) and general budget statutes (Chapters 129 and 200, Florida Statutes) governing capital appropriations.

## Frequently Asked Follow-Up Questions

**Why does a project take multiple years to complete?** Design, permitting, environmental review, procurement, and construction phases for infrastructure projects commonly span more than one fiscal year.

**How is a specific capital project funded?** Through a combination shown for that specific project — current revenue, grants, and/or debt — detailed in its CIP entry alongside its timeline, location, and estimated cost.

**Can a resident search for a specific capital project?** Yes, via the CIP Project Search page.

**Does every capital project use debt financing?** No — many are funded from current revenue or grants; debt is used selectively for larger projects, as explained in Why Government Uses Debt.

- Capital projects are planned through a multi-year Capital Improvement Plan, not the single-year operating budget.

- Funding typically blends current revenue, grants, and sometimes debt financing.
- Residents can look up individual project status and funding through the CIP Project Search tool.

A capital project is formally defined as an effort to acquire or improve an asset with an anticipated life exceeding one year — including land acquisition, new construction, structural repair or improvement, engineering and design, and equipment acquisition or repair — and each project entry in the CIP documents its expected completion time frame, location, description, estimated total cost, and proposed financing method. Different capital needs draw on different funds depending on their nature: transportation projects primarily draw on the Transportation Fund and related grant and impact-fee revenue; Sheriff's Office capital needs draw on the Sheriff Fund; broader county facility and infrastructure projects draw on the dedicated Capital Projects Fund. Residents can look up the funding source, timeline, and status of a specific capital project through the county's CIP Project Search tool, and can review fund-specific capital schedules for the Capital Projects Fund, Sheriff Fund, Tourist Development Fund, and Transportation Fund individually.
