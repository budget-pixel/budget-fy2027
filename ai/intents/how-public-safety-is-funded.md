# Intent: How is public safety funded in Walton County?

## Question

How does Walton County fund the Sheriff's Office, emergency management, and other public safety services?

## Short Answer

Public safety is funded primarily through the Sheriff Fund (fines, forfeitures, grants, and transfers under Florida Statutes) and the General Fund's ad valorem tax revenue, with additional support from the E911 Fund (911 phone charges) and the Law Enforcement Trust Fund (forfeiture proceeds restricted to law enforcement purposes).

## Detailed Explanation

In Walton County, the Sheriff's Office is a separately elected Constitutional Officer responsible for law enforcement, corrections, court services, animal control, fire rescue, and 911 dispatch — a broader scope than in many counties, where some of these functions sit with separate agencies. Its budget is appropriated by the Board of County Commissioners but managed independently by the Sheriff once approved.

The Sheriff Fund accounts for fines, forfeitures, grants, and transfers supporting Sheriff's Office operations in accordance with Florida Statutes. It is one of the County's six major governmental funds, distinct from the General Fund, though the General Fund also transfers ad valorem tax-supported funding to help finance Sheriff's Office operations.

Other public safety funding sources include:

- **E911 Fund**: phone charges assessed specifically for emergency communications infrastructure and 911 dispatch operations.
- **Law Enforcement Trust Fund**: proceeds from law enforcement forfeitures, restricted under Florida Statutes to law enforcement-related purposes.
- **General Fund**: supports Emergency Management, Code Compliance, the Building Department, and the Medical Examiner (a state-appointed position whose costs the county helps fund under Florida law).

## Why This Matters

Public safety, particularly the Sheriff's Office, is typically the largest single expenditure activity in a Florida county budget. Understanding its funding mix — dedicated fund revenue, forfeitures, grants, and General Fund support — clarifies why "the Sheriff's budget" is not simply one line paid from one tax.

## Legal Context

The Sheriff's Office operates under Florida Statutes governing county law enforcement and Constitutional Officer budget procedures. Forfeiture proceeds in the Law Enforcement Trust Fund are governed by Florida's forfeiture statutes restricting their use to law enforcement purposes.

## Frequently Asked Follow-Up Questions

**Does the Sheriff's Office run animal control and fire rescue in Walton County?** Yes — the Walton County Sheriff's Office has taken on operation of the animal control facility/units and Walton County Fire Rescue Services, in addition to traditional law enforcement, corrections, and court security functions.

**Can Law Enforcement Trust Fund money be spent on non-law-enforcement needs?** No — it is restricted under Florida forfeiture law to law enforcement-related purposes.

**Who pays for 911 dispatch specifically?** A dedicated phone-charge revenue source flowing into the E911 Fund, separate from the Sheriff Fund, even though the Sheriff's Office operates the E911 Communications Center.

**Does the Board control how the Sheriff spends the Sheriff Fund?** The Board approves the total appropriation; the Sheriff, as an independently elected Constitutional Officer, manages internal line-item spending, hiring, and policy.

## Key Takeaways

- The Sheriff's Office covers law enforcement, corrections, court services, animal control, fire rescue, and 911 dispatch in Walton County.
- Its funding blends the dedicated Sheriff Fund, General Fund support, E911 fees, and restricted forfeiture proceeds.
- The Board appropriates the Sheriff's total budget but does not control internal spending decisions.
- Other public safety functions (Emergency Management, Code Compliance, Building Department, Medical Examiner) are separately funded, mostly through the General Fund.
