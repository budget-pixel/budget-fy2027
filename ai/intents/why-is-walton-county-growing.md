# Intent: Why is Walton County growing?

## Question

Why is Walton County experiencing growth, and how does the County track and manage it?

## Short Answer

Walton County's coastal geography (26 miles of Gulf coastline, the Scenic Highway 30A/South Walton area) and its dual inland-coastal economy make it an attractive place to visit, retire, and relocate to — and growth is managed through the County's Comprehensive Plan, Land Development Code, and concurrency management process administered by the Planning Department, rather than left unmanaged.

## Detailed Explanation

Walton County includes both a large inland area rooted in agriculture, timber heritage, and small-town civic life, and a nationally known coastal tourist destination along Scenic Highway 30A and South Walton. This combination — natural amenities (Gulf coastline, coastal dune lakes, forests, lakes), a strong visitor economy, and Florida's broader growth trends — are widely understood factors drawing new residents and development to coastal Florida Panhandle counties generally. This site's official budget materials do not publish specific population or growth-rate statistics, so this document intentionally does not cite a figure it cannot verify from Walton County's own published data; see the Statistical & Supplemental Information page for any official statistical detail the County publishes.

What the budget materials do document clearly is how the County *manages* growth once it occurs: the Walton County Planning & Development Services Department provides land use planning advice to the Board of County Commissioners and is responsible for implementing, maintaining, and updating the Comprehensive Plan and Land Development Code, concurrency management (including implementation of the Mobility Plan), floodplain management, and development review in unincorporated Walton County — ensuring proposed development complies with adopted codes, policies, and regulations.

Financially, growth is addressed through impact fees and the Mobility Fee Fund, which charge new development for the infrastructure burden it creates, under Florida's rational nexus legal framework — see [How Impact Fees Work](how-impact-fees-work.md). See [How Growth Affects the Budget](how-growth-affects-the-budget.md) for the fuller financial-planning picture.

## Why This Matters

Understanding growth as a *managed* process — not simply something happening to the county — clarifies why development review, concurrency requirements, and impact fees exist: they are the tools the County uses to make sure infrastructure and services keep pace with new development.

## Legal Context

Florida's growth management statutes require local comprehensive plans and land development regulations; concurrency management requires necessary infrastructure to be available concurrent with development impacts.
