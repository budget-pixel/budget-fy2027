# Public Safety

## Purpose

This document explains how Walton County organizes and funds public safety services.

## Overview

"Public Safety" is one of Walton County's eight expenditure activity classifications, spanning several departments and offices rather than a single budget line. See [Expenditure Guide](expenditure-guide.md) for how activity classifications work.

## What Public Safety Covers in Walton County

- **Sheriff's Office**: law enforcement (uniformed patrol, criminal investigations, crime scene investigation, K-9 units), corrections (detention facility operations, inmate housing, medical/food services, transportation), court services (bailiffs, courtroom security, jury protection), and — specific to Walton County — animal control and Walton County Fire Rescue Services, plus the E911 Communications Center. See [Constitutional Officers](constitutional-officers.md).
- **Emergency Management**: disaster management 24/7 using a "Whole Community Approach," operating the Emergency Operations Center (EOC) as the hub for communications, command, and coordination during disasters.
- **Code Compliance**: enforcement of codes, regulations, and ordinances protecting community aesthetics, property values, health, safety, and quality of life.
- **Building Department**: regulatory oversight and support throughout the construction process to ensure safety, compliance, and integrity of construction projects.
- **Medical Examiner**: state-appointed official determining cause of death under specified circumstances, per Florida law.
- **South Walton Fire / State Fire Control**: interlocal-agreement-funded emergency medical services (South Walton Fire District) and state fire prevention/control assessments.

## How It's Funded

Public safety funding is not a single revenue stream — it blends:

- **Sheriff Fund**: fines, forfeitures, grants, and transfers dedicated to Sheriff's Office operations.
- **General Fund**: ad valorem tax revenue supporting Emergency Management, Code Compliance, Building Department, and Medical Examiner costs, plus transfers to the Sheriff Fund.
- **E911 Fund**: dedicated phone-service charges funding 911 dispatch infrastructure.
- **Law Enforcement Trust Fund**: forfeiture proceeds restricted under Florida Statutes to law enforcement purposes.

See [How Public Safety Is Funded](intents/how-public-safety-is-funded.md) for the full explanation.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is public safety the largest expenditure activity in the budget?** It is typically one of the largest, since it is personnel-intensive and covers 24/7 operations — see the Summary of Expenses (live page) for the current total.

**Does the Board control the Sheriff's Office budget line by line?** No — the Board approves the total appropriation; the Sheriff, as an independently elected Constitutional Officer, manages internal spending, hiring, and policy.

**Who funds South Walton Fire's EMS services?** The County, through an interlocal agreement with the South Walton Fire District covering emergency and non-emergency medical services and transport.

## Common Misconceptions

- **"Public Safety" means only the Sheriff's Office.** It also includes Emergency Management, Code Compliance, the Building Department, and the Medical Examiner.
- **"The County runs its own fire department."** In southern Walton County, fire/EMS is provided by the separate South Walton Fire District under an interlocal agreement; the Sheriff's Office separately operates Walton County Fire Rescue Services.
